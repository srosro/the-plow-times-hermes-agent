# Discovery — $0–1M ARR

## Signals
- $0–1M ARR, and not yet 5–10 customers who will take a reference call.
- The founder builds, sells, pitches, closes, implements and supports: "the ultimate individual contributor."
- No repeatable sales motion; every deal feels bespoke.

## Focus first
- Reach product-market fit: a painkiller, not a vitamin, plus 5–10 referenceable customers.
- Talk to "not just a handful but tens or hundreds" of customers before pitching investors.
- Sell founder-led by teaching and insight: show buyers a new way to see the problem instead of qualifying them out.
- Name the one to three needs you solve, so every conversation confirms a need first.
- Surround yourself with great advisers and early hires where you lack experience.
- Pay yourself a living salary; no founder succeeds while holding a second job.

## Do not focus on
- Hiring a sales team to figure out the motion for you.
- Building the GTM machine: org design, capacity planning, forecasting.
- Pitching VCs before you have talked to scores of customers.
- Setting arbitrary raise deadlines or manufacturing FOMO.

## Benchmarks
- Product-market fit for enterprise software usually lands at $0–1M ARR.
- 5–10 referenceable customers, depending on GTM model and target customer.
- Tens or hundreds of customer conversations before the first investor pitch.

## Exit criteria
- A painkiller product, 5–10 referenceable customers, and similar customers landing on a repeatable message. Next: Blueprint.
