# Scale — $10M+ ARR

## Signals
- $10M+ ARR, scaling toward $100M, with multiple ramped reps.
- Org design, capacity planning, enablement, pipeline plans and forecasting are the daily problems.
- Everything still routes through the founder, and the bottleneck is growing.

## Focus first
- Replicate the motion with a repeatable ramp model, and change your job from doing the work to designing the machine.
- Build the team: hire great people for the jobs you used to do.
- Prioritize relentlessly; at scale, focus, not effort, is the constraint.
- Over-communicate the vision and priorities "until you're blue in the face."
- Personally own 1–3 critical initiatives per quarter and explicitly delegate the rest.
- List what depends on you and redesign one so it does not; hire, delegate or automate recurring work, AI agents included.
- Plan the year top-down: mission, a 12–24 month objective and strategy, then a financial plan grounded in pipeline, churn, margins and runway.
- Write 3–5 annual priorities, each beginning with a verb and ending "from X to Y by Z," ideally with a lead measure.
- Give each quarterly initiative one executive owner, and review lead and lag measures on a fixed cadence.
- Add a theme for the year: the plan motivates execs and the board, a theme motivates the team (Gigya's was #GigyaLeads).

## Do not focus on
- Staying the hero and the bottleneck: "Build the machine that builds the business."
- Letting effort substitute for focus.
- Saying the vision once and assuming it landed.
- Motivating the team with the financial plan alone.
- Writing initiatives you cannot state as "from X to Y by Z" with an owner.

## Benchmarks
- Reps at ~80% of quota.
- 2–3x new ACV growth, which is what carries a company to $100M+.
- The CEO owns 1–3 initiatives per quarter; the company runs on 3–5 annual priorities.

## Exit criteria
- A ramp model at ~80% quota and 2–3x ACV growth, an org that runs without the founder in every loop, and analyst-recognized category leadership.
