# Pivot — crisis at any stage

## Signals
- An acute shock: a security incident or outage, a market re-rating of your category, or a technology shift such as agentic AI threatening the model.
- Missed milestones mean the next round will not happen on prior terms.
- A competitor with a fraction of your revenue is valued far above you.

## Focus first
- Operational crisis: assess fast what you know and do not; go to the front-line people with the facts, not only direct reports.
- Communicate early and often with the board, employees, customers and partners; do not wait for certainty to share bad news.
- "Take" decisions rather than "make" them; in a crisis the CEO does the work personally.
- Turn the crisis into change: after Gigya's hack came a CISO, more security budget, root-cause fixes and a reusable crisis playbook.
- Market or model crisis: stop defending the status quo and re-found the business around AI-native outcomes.
- Plan around 2–3x revenue, not 10x; that number changes every fundraising, M&A and burn decision.
- If your value may sit below the liquidation-preference stack, accept it; that frees you to take extreme action.
- Use what a newcomer lacks: distribution through existing contracts, data from your platform, and trust.
- Pick a path: re-found around an AI-native outcome, get in the path of AI demand, or make the system of record a system of action.
- Build AI-native, with an AI-native team, on durable moats: a graph of decisions and outcomes, regulation, a live-data flywheel, earned trust, security.
- If none of those paths is bearable, sell the company; the status quo is the one option that does not work.

## Do not focus on
- Waiting for perfect information before communicating.
- Relying only on direct reports for the facts.
- Protecting the status quo: "the only option that really doesn't work."
- Anchoring on peak 10x–30x revenue multiples.
- Getting angry at better-valued AI-native competitors instead of curious.
- Expecting the transition to be easy: "there is a reason for the metaphor 'burn the boats.'"

## Benchmarks
- Plan around 2–3x revenue; he frames multiples as falling from a ~30x 2021 peak to a ~3–4x median.
- An AI-native dev team is roughly 30% of your current team size.
- An AI-native GTM team carries higher quotas, new pricing models and forward-deployed engineers.
- Intercom's Fin went from $0 to $100M+ ARR "in a few years."

## Exit criteria
- Operational: root causes fixed, customers kept, and the crisis playbook written down.
- Market or model: the company is committed to one of the three paths, or sold; not holding the status quo.

## His record here
- Three pivots at Gigya (social advertising, then social infrastructure, then customer identity), and a down round "the hardest round I ever raised."
- A 2014 hack by the Syrian Electronic Army, handled without losing, by his recollection, a single customer.
- His operational-crisis writing rests on that one case; for co-founder disputes or key-customer churn, present it as general leadership advice, not his prescription.
